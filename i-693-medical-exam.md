# Form I-693, Medical Examination and Vaccination Record: What It Is and How to Submit It

**General information only.** This article explains Form I-693 in plain English for a person applying for adjustment of status. It is not legal advice, and it does not tell any person what to do in their own case. Immigration cases depend on the facts of each person's situation. A person who is unsure about their case should talk to an immigration attorney or a DOJ-accredited representative before filing anything.

## The short answer

Form I-693 is the Report of Immigration Medical Examination and Vaccination Record. It establishes that the applicant is not inadmissible on health-related grounds under section 212(a)(1) of the Immigration and Nationality Act (INA). In plain terms, it is the form that records the result of the immigration medical exam and the applicant's vaccination record. Since December 2, 2024, the form must be submitted together with Form I-485, the application for adjustment of status. If Form I-693 is not submitted with Form I-485, USCIS may reject the I-485. After the exam, the civil surgeon must give the completed form to the applicant in a sealed envelope, and the applicant must not accept it if it is not sealed. The applicant, not the civil surgeon, submits Form I-693 to USCIS by mail with Form I-485. The current edition of the form is 01/20/25. ## What Form I-693 is

Form I-693 is the Report of Immigration Medical Examination and Vaccination Record. A civil surgeon is the doctor who performs the immigration medical exam inside the United States. The civil surgeon completes the form after the exam. The form has two parts to its purpose. First, it records the medical examination. Second, it records the applicant's vaccinations.

The form establishes that the applicant is not inadmissible on health-related grounds under section 212(a)(1) of the INA. "Inadmissible" means that a person is not allowed to enter or remain in the United States under the immigration law. Health-related grounds are one of the reasons a person can be found inadmissible. Form I-693 is the document that shows the result of the medical exam and the vaccination record, so that USCIS can decide the health-related part of the case.

The form is completed by a civil surgeon, not by the applicant. The applicant goes to the civil surgeon for the exam, and the civil surgeon fills in the form. The applicant's role is to attend the exam, to receive the completed form in a sealed envelope, and to submit that sealed envelope to USCIS with Form I-485.

## Submit it with Form I-485

Effective December 2, 2024, an applicant must submit Form I-693, or a partial Form I-693 such as the Vaccination Record, together with Form I-485. Form I-485 is the Application to Register Permanent Residence or Adjust Status. It is the main form for a person who is applying for a green card from inside the United States.

The rule is clear. The medical form goes in with the adjustment application. If Form I-693 is not submitted with Form I-485, USCIS may reject the I-485. A rejection means the application is sent back and is not accepted for processing. This can cause delay. Because the rule changed on December 2, 2024, a person who is preparing an adjustment application should check the USCIS Form I-693 page or talk to an immigration attorney or a DOJ-accredited representative if they are not sure whether the rule applies to them.

A partial Form I-693, such as the Vaccination Record, can be submitted with Form I-485 in some situations. The next section explains who submits a partial form.

## Who submits a partial Form I-693

Not every applicant submits a full Form I-693. Some applicants submit a partial Form I-693. A partial form means that only some parts of the form are completed and submitted. The rules for a partial form depend on the applicant's situation and on whether they already had an immigration medical exam outside the United States.

### Refugees and derivative asylees

Refugees and derivative asylees who already had an immigration medical exam by a panel physician outside the United States submit a partial Form I-693 when they file for adjustment of status. A panel physician is a doctor outside the United States who is authorized to perform immigration medical exams. A refugee is a person who filed Form I-590 or Form I-730. A derivative asylee is a person who filed Form I-730 as the family member of a person who was granted asylum.

For these applicants, the partial Form I-693 includes Parts 1 through 5, Part 7, and Part 10, which is the Vaccination Record. Parts 1 through 5 cover the applicant's information and the civil surgeon's findings. Part 7 is the civil surgeon's certification. Part 10 is the Vaccination Record. These applicants submit this partial form when they file for adjustment of status.

### K visa holders

A K-1, K-2, K-3, or K-4 visa holder who had an immigration medical exam abroad no more than one year before filing also submits a partial Form I-693 if the vaccination record was not properly completed. K-1 is the fiance(e) of a U.S. citizen. K-2 is the child of a K-1. K-3 is the spouse of a U.S. citizen. K-4 is the child of a K-3. The key point is the timing. The exam abroad must have been no more than one year before the person files. If the vaccination record was not properly completed at the time of that exam, the person submits a partial Form I-693.

### Afghan nationals under Operation Allies Welcome

Afghan nationals under Operation Allies Welcome who had an immigration medical exam abroad also submit a partial Form I-693 if the vaccination record was not properly completed. Operation Allies Welcome is the U.S. government program for Afghan nationals who were brought to the United States. The same condition applies as for K visa holders. If the vaccination record was not properly completed at the time of the exam abroad, the person submits a partial Form I-693.

### Class A medical conditions

A Class A medical condition may require a new exam. A Class A medical condition is a medical condition that the immigration law treats as a ground of inadmissibility. If an applicant has a Class A medical condition, USCIS may require a new medical exam. A person in this situation should talk to an immigration attorney or a DOJ-accredited representative, because the rules are specific to the person's medical situation.

## Which edition of the form to use

USCIS updates its forms from time to time, and it is important to use the edition that USCIS currently accepts. The current edition of Form I-693 is 01/20/25.

The edition that is accepted depends on when the civil surgeon signs the form. If the civil surgeon signs the form on or before July 2, 2025, USCIS accepts the 03/09/23 edition or the 01/20/25 edition. If the civil surgeon signs the form on July 3, 2025 or later, USCIS accepts only the 01/20/25 edition.

The date that matters is the date the civil surgeon signs, not the date the applicant files. This means the applicant should make sure the civil surgeon uses the right edition and signs on the right date. If the wrong edition is used, USCIS may not accept the form. A person who is not sure which edition applies should check the USCIS Form I-693 page or talk to an immigration attorney or a DOJ-accredited representative.

## The sealed envelope

After the exam, the civil surgeon must give the completed Form I-693 to the applicant in a sealed envelope. The word "sealed" means the envelope is closed and cannot be opened without breaking the seal. The applicant must not accept the form if the envelope is not sealed. This is a strict rule.

USCIS returns the form if the envelope is not sealed, or if it has been opened or altered. "Altered" means changed in any way. If the envelope has been opened, even by accident, USCIS will return the form. The applicant would then need to get a new sealed envelope from the civil surgeon. This can cause delay.

The applicant, not the civil surgeon, must submit Form I-693 to USCIS. The applicant submits it by mail, together with Form I-485. The civil surgeon does not send the form to USCIS. The applicant is responsible for keeping the sealed envelope safe and for mailing it with the adjustment application.

In plain terms, the steps are:

- The applicant has the medical exam with a civil surgeon.
- The civil surgeon completes Form I-693 and puts it in a sealed envelope.
- The civil surgeon gives the sealed envelope to the applicant.
- The applicant checks that the envelope is sealed. If it is not sealed, the applicant does not accept it.
- The applicant mails the sealed envelope to USCIS together with Form I-485.

The applicant should not open the envelope. The applicant should not let anyone else open it. The envelope must reach USCIS sealed.

## Fees

USCIS charges a filing fee for Form I-693. This article does not state the fee amount, because fees can change. To find the current fee, see the USCIS Fee Schedule page. A person should check the fee on the USCIS website before filing, and should not rely on a fee amount found in an older article or on another website.

The exam itself is separate from the filing fee. The civil surgeon sets the fee for the medical exam. Exam fees are set by the civil surgeon and vary by civil surgeon. This means the cost of the exam can be different from one civil surgeon to another. A person who wants to know the cost of the exam should ask the civil surgeon directly.

## How long the form is valid

This article does not state a specific number of months or years for how long Form I-693 is valid. The validity of the form is governed by the USCIS Policy Manual, Volume 8, Part B, Chapter 4. A person who wants to know how long the form is valid should read that chapter of the Policy Manual on the USCIS website, or talk to an immigration attorney or a DOJ-accredited representative. The Policy Manual is the official source for the validity rules, and it is the place to check for the current rule.

## When to talk to an attorney or a DOJ-accredited representative

Form I-693 involves a medical exam, a vaccination record, and rules that depend on a person's immigration history. There are situations where a person should talk to an immigration attorney or a DOJ-accredited representative before filing. These include:

- If the person already had a medical exam outside the United States and is not sure whether they need a full form or a partial form.
- If the person is a refugee, a derivative asylee, a K visa holder, or an Afghan national under Operation Allies Welcome and is not sure which parts of the form apply.
- If the person has a Class A medical condition, or is not sure whether they have one.
- If the person is not sure which edition of the form the civil surgeon should use, or whether the signature date matters.
- If the sealed envelope was opened, damaged, or altered, and the person is not sure what to do.
- If the person has questions about how long the form is valid.

An immigration attorney or a DOJ-accredited representative can give advice about a person's own case. This article cannot do that. It only explains what the form is and what USCIS asks for.
